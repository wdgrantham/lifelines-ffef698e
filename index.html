How to read the marks

Color is paired with shape. These marks are teaching symbols, not a coded diagnosis of anyone’s life.

  • Black circle — deathThe death of someone close: a father, a father-in-law, family or friends.
  • Green square — want, hunger, or illnessNot enough to eat, exhaustion, or sickness — like the hunger at the broken bow (1 Ne 16:18–20) or sick-beds of grief (18:17–18).
  • Pink heart — heartacheGrief, fear, mourning, strain, or a painful moment in the family.
  • Red diamond — harm by anotherViolence inflicted by someone else. The person harmed is not at fault.
  • Ringed dot — revelationA vision, dream, witness, or direction from the Lord. Mothers receive revelation too (1 Ne 5:8).
  • Open ring — turning pointA step taken in the story: asking a father, finding food, leaving with Nephi.
  • Gold star — spouseMarriage. Two lines travel together; neither line is absorbed into the other.
  • Blue star — childrenA child’s arrival, like Jacob and Joseph born in the wilderness (1 Ne 18:7). Color marks generation, not gender.
  • Pink star — grandchildrenLehi’s grandchildren, whom he blessed (2 Ne 4:3–9).
  • Shield — sacred momentsAn altar and offerings (1 Ne 2:7; 5:9), a father’s blessings (2 Ne 1–4), consecration to serve (2 Ne 5:26), and the temple Nephi built (2 Ne 5:16). They connect people to Christ; they do not prevent hardship.

Positions are schematic: time runs left to right but is not to scale, and dates are approximate.

How to use this teaching

This is one continuous picture, not a stack of separate slides. The camera pans and zooms through a shared world of lifelines. It follows Lehi’s family from the Book of Mormon (1 Nephi 1 – 2 Nephi 5), then pulls back to a field of the generations that followed. Every mark cites its verses; time is schematic and not to scale.
